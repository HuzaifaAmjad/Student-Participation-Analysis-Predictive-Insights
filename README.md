# Student-Participation-Analysis-Predictive-Insights
The dataset contains 8,558 learner participation records and 16 original attributes, including demographic, academic, geographic, opportunity, registration, and participation information. Exploratory analysis showed that Internships were the most common opportunity category, representing 5,421 records, or 63.34% of total participation, while Courses represented 23.80%. Internship opportunities also showed a high rejection rate, with 65.84% of applications rejected and 21.49% progressing to Team Allocated.
Participation patterns also varied over time. Internship activity increased strongly between August and January, with particularly high registration activity around January and February 2024. Geographic analysis showed that learners came from 70 countries; however, participation was highly concentrated in the United States, India, and Nigeria, which together represented 88.48% of all participation.
Four machine learning models were evaluated: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost. Random Forest achieved the strongest predictive performance, with 86.04% accuracy, 92.35% precision, 77.06% recall, an F1-score of 84.01%, and a ROC-AUC score of 0.9320. Feature importance analysis identified Opportunity Category and Opportunity Duration as the strongest predictors of learner participation, followed by variables such as Signup Month, Institution Name, and Current/Intended Major.
Based on these findings, the project recommends expanding high-demand opportunities, improving learner-opportunity matching, strengthening learner engagement and retention, using historical participation patterns for programme planning, expanding outreach to underrepresented regions, and using predictive analytics to support decision-making.
A KPI and success measurement framework is also proposed to help Excelerate monitor participation, programme outcomes, geographic diversity, and predictive model performance over time. This framework is designed to support continuous improvement and ensure that future decisions remain evidence-based and measurable.
